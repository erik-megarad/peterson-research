Peterson Research is a growing collection covering Gary L. Peterson's research.
It covers Essential Dekker, economical solutions, circular extrema election, concurrent reading and multi-reader atomic values. The selected
positive and negative results for those five papers are complete within the scopes
in their guides. This status describes the current contents; it neither closes
the collection to further papers nor means every claim in these papers has
been proved. Version 1.1.0 identifies the published artifact; no tag or GitHub
release is implied.

## Limitations

[Essential Dekker](docs/essential-dekker.md) preserves the distinction between
a written obstruction for the printed inequality, checked scan arithmetic,
and a checked infinite starvation witness after a local correction. The
corrected model is safe but does not have the refuted request-service property.

[Economical solutions](docs/economical-solutions.md) preserves claim-specific
source versions, the automatic reset/abort failure model, original request
identity and conditional scheduling/completion premises. Algorithms 5–7 use
reviewed outline expansions. Algorithm 7's publication FIFO refutation has a
specific chosen boundary. Economy covers exclude private memory; the lower
bound uses failures; solo work begins at initialization. The guide lists all
unestablished and outside-scope claims, including Algorithm 4 and timing.

[Circular election](docs/circular-election.md) retains reliable FIFO delivery and local fairness for eventual election, and counts actual sends through first announcement with uniform constant 3.

[Concurrent reading](docs/concurrent-reading.md) retains overlapping raw buffers, atomic controls, the pending-writer history extension and continued own scheduling with raw-operation termination. Its common order is per finite history.

[Multi-reader atomic values](docs/multi-reader-atomic.md) reports two negative atomicity results for the normalized Figure 1 construction under regular-register semantics. Its original and restricted completion theorems and primitive budgets are separate positive results. No repaired construction or stronger FC contract is selected.

The sources have restoration and unavailable-erratum limitations. Kernel checks
establish formal propositions, not paper faithfulness, weak-memory correctness,
wall-clock bounds or executable refinement. MIT covers applicable project
rights, not source papers or dependencies. See the [overview](README.md),
[declarations](docs/claims.md) and [verification guide](docs/verification.md).
